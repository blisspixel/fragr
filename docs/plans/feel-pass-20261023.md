# Feel pass: fast arena movement, grounded aim

Status: **implemented**, 2026-10-24, on branch `feat/feel-pass` (rebased on
`main` after wave 1). Measured before and after below; Windows and human play
not yet run.

## Decisions this plan carries out

Nick, 2026-10-23:

- **Movement: "Fast arena + grounded aim".** Quake, Unreal Tournament and Doom
  speed, air control, rocket-jump and dodge tricks, with Counter-Strike crisp
  stops, acceleration and responsiveness: "buttery smooth like Counter-Strike
  nailed it".
- **Weapon handling: "Arena accuracy + kick".** The first shot goes exactly
  where you aim. Visual kick and camera punch carry the feel. A small learnable
  spread grows only on sustained automatic fire. Skill is aim and tracking.

## Goal

Make moving and shooting feel excellent while the Rust server stays the only
authority. Measure the change with the same rendered probe before and after,
and freeze the movement constants in one documented place.

## Non-goals

- No tick-rate change (the 20 Hz server stays), no transport change, no new
  scripting runtime, no backward compatibility shims (pre-1.0).
- No change to campaign enemy movement timing or enemy weapon dispersion:
  authored encounters keep their tuned speeds, so `CAMPAIGN_RULES_REVISION`
  stays 4.
- No new art or audio purchases; existing assets only. $0 spend.

## Research, 2026-10-23

Primary sources read for this pass, with the values used below:

| Source | What it settles |
|---|---|
| ioquake3 `code/game/bg_pmove.c` | Quake 3 movement: `pm_accelerate` 10, `pm_airaccelerate` 1, `pm_friction` 6, `pm_stopspeed` 100 u/s. Friction drop is `max(speed, stopspeed) * friction * dt`; accelerate adds `min(accel * dt * wishspeed, wishspeed - dot(v, wishdir))`. A jump leaves `PM_WalkMove` before ground friction, which is what lets a hop keep its speed. Jump needs a fresh press (`PMF_JUMP_HELD`). |
| ioquake3 `code/cgame/cg_local.h`, `cg_view.c` | View feel timings: damage kick deflects over 100 ms and returns over 400 ms, landing dips over 150 ms and recovers over 300 ms, step smoothing 200 ms, muzzle flash 20 ms. |
| Valve Source SDK 2013 `gamemovement.cpp` | Counter-Strike lineage: the same friction/stopspeed and accelerate formulas; air acceleration clamps wish speed to an air cap while accelerating by the uncapped value (the surf and strafe gain); jump refuses a held button ("don't pogo stick"). Valve's wiki lists CS:GO `sv_friction` 5.2. |
| Riot, "Peeking into VALORANT's Netcode" | Fixed-step movement prediction, server-wins correction of the predicting player only, late input assumed held, rewind of the world to what the shooter saw (bounded), target of one buffered movement frame on clients. |
| Overwatch GDC 2017 (session description and secondary summaries) | Fixed command frames, the client running ahead of the server by about half the RTT plus a frame, prediction of the local player's own actions. |
| CS2 sub-tick (Valve announcement as quoted by secondary sources) | Inputs carry their time inside the tick, so the outcome does not depend on tick boundaries. fragr cannot timestamp inputs on the wire in this pass; the client instead removes its own frame of delay and phase-locks its prediction clock. |

Unit note: one fragr unit is one metre; one Quake or Hammer unit is treated as
one inch. Counter-Strike's 250 u/s knife speed is 6.35 m/s, Quake 3's 320 u/s
is 8.13 m/s.

## Audit, 2026-10-23 (before any change)

Read from source at `e4cec37` and measured with the new rendered probe
(`tools/qa_feel.sh`, Arena Duel, no bots, Linux, Radeon 780M, loaded host):

1. **Movement model.** `movement::live_step` sets horizontal velocity
   immediately from the keys every 20 Hz tick. There is no acceleration,
   friction, air control or momentum: the server discards velocity each tick
   (`vx: 0.0` into the step). Starts and stops are binary, a jump cannot be
   steered or carried, and a rocket can only push upward.
2. **Local view between ticks.** `LocalPrediction` keeps a second
   free-running "visual pose" integrated with partial ticks. A correction
   offset is measured against the committed tick pose, not the drawn one, and
   applied to the drawn one, then clamped at 0.25 m. When the committed pose
   moves a whole step (re-anchored step clock after an Ack), the drawn eye
   slows to about a quarter of its speed for roughly 70 ms. The probe shows
   steady-strafe frame speed variation of 14 to 27 percent and these dips.
3. **Prediction clock.** Step windows re-anchor to each Ack's arrival frame,
   so the client's tick phase jitters with frame and delivery timing.
4. **Aim sent one frame late.** `GameManager._process` (parent) sends the
   Action before the camera (child) applies that frame's mouse motion. The
   drawn view turns that frame; the server and prediction get the yaw one
   frame later, so a flick-and-click fires at the previous frame's aim.
5. **Weapons.** Every shot uses the full table cone (`spread_radians`),
   including the first: the Tack 1.7 degrees, Flechette 2.6, Repeater 2.0,
   Rail 0.7. A centred first shot can miss. The first-person kick is a 2D
   viewmodel offset only; the camera never punches. Hit markers, a hit chime
   and floating damage exist.
6. **Remote targets.** Others render two ticks (100 ms) behind the newest
   Snapshot and hitscan has no rewind (`buttery-controls.md` stage 5). A
   centred shot on a fighter strafing at full speed lands about half a metre
   behind the body that was drawn.

## Design

### Movement (server, mirror, goldens)

One fighter model replaces both the immediate live step and the older
accelerated 60 Hz step. It runs at any `dt`; the live server uses 20 Hz and
the second golden file pins 60 Hz behavior. Participants (humans, agents, rule
bots, the campaign participant) use it. Authored campaign actors keep the
immediate step at their unchanged reference speed.

Per step: wish direction from the keys and client yaw; if grounded and not
jumping, Quake/Source friction; then ground acceleration (grounded) or air
acceleration (airborne, clamped so air strafing cannot push horizontal speed
past an air cap, while faster momentum from a blast is kept); then the
existing collision, step, jump, gravity and landing integration. A jump on the
landing tick skips friction, so a held jump carries speed. Velocity persists
between server ticks and is the Ack's `vx`/`vz`, which prediction already
replays from.

All constants live in one block at the top of `server/src/movement.rs`,
mirrored in `client/scripts/movement.gd` and recorded in both golden files.

### Local presentation

- Mouse motion is applied to aim when the event arrives, so the Action sent in
  the same frame carries it.
- The drawn local pose interpolates between committed predicted tick states.
  No second integrator.
- The step clock is locked to the server's input cutoff, not to Ack arrival.
  Each Ack names the newest Action its tick used, so that Action's send time is
  a lower bound on the cutoff, and the next Action's send time or the Ack's own
  arrival (nothing sent after it reached that tick) is an upper bound. The
  midpoint of the tightest bounds over a 40 Ack window sets the schedule. A
  press or release then lands in the tick the client predicted for it at any
  steady latency. While the body moves the schedule slews 1 ms per Ack; at
  rest, where moving it moves nothing on screen, it takes the estimate at once.
- The eye is drawn half a tick (25 ms) behind that input clock. A release
  revises a tick the eye has not finished drawing, so the eye never runs past
  where the body stops.
- Any change to the presented pose that is not motion (an Ack correction, a
  revised input inside the open tick) becomes an offset that decays over
  about 60 ms instead of a jump. While the body moves the offset never pulls
  the eye backwards. After a release the offset glides: it shrinks with the
  distance the body has left to its predicted rest, so the eye decelerates
  into the stop and arrives with the body. Only a teleport snaps.

### Weapons

- Participants fire their first shot along the exact aim ray. Automatic fire
  inside a short recovery window grows a small seeded cone per weapon up to the
  previous table value (`WeaponType::bloom_per_shot`: Tack 0.25, Flechette
  0.2, Repeater 0.1, Arc 0.15 of the full cone a shot; recovery constants next
  to the weapon table in `protocol.rs`). Rail, Sniper and Rocket stay exact.
  The Scatter keeps its pellet cone. Campaign encounters keep the table cone.
- First-person camera punch per weapon (`spectator_cam.gd` `PUNCH_DEGREES`):
  a pitch kick and small roll on a critically damped spring, peak in about
  25 ms and back in about 0.2 s, presentation only; aim is unchanged. A hard
  landing dips the eye. Reduced motion disables both. A kill shot shows a
  larger danger-coloured hit marker with a lower thud.

### Done and not done in this pass

- Done: the ground dodge on its own rebindable key (Left Shift), through the
  shared step, both goldens, the Action and the Ack.
- Done: a rocket's own blast throws a fighter away from it as well as up
  (capped at 14 m/s), so a rocket jump carries.
- Not done: hitscan rewind to the drawn remote time. The October 23 playtest
  round lists lag compensation as a non-goal; it stays stage 5 of
  [buttery controls](buttery-controls.md).
- Not done: timeline interpolation of other fighters (buttery controls stage
  4), the dodge recovery penalty and double-tap dodge.

## Protocol or API changes

- `Action.dodge`: optional press, latched like a jump tap.
- Ack `movement.version` 2 for the fighter model (1 for the campaign's
  immediate step) and `movement.dodge_cooldown`. A client refuses to replay a
  version it does not know. No compatibility path, per the pre-1.0 rule.
- MCP `act` accepts `dodge`. `docs/protocol.md` and `agent-adapter/README.md`
  carry the contract, the movement constants table and the weapon dispersion.

## Results, 2026-10-24

`tools/qa_feel.sh` (the same probe on both builds), Arena Duel with no bots,
loopback, Linux, Radeon 780M, 1280x720 window, host load about 30 on 16
threads from other work. Before is `main` at `0fefa33` with the probe copied
in, three runs; after is this branch, six runs. Medians with the range. The
wall clock times include the loaded host's frame pacing (p99 frames of 20 to
37 ms), so single runs vary widely.

The closed-loop case in `test_local_prediction.gd` covers what the rendered
probe cannot: steady links from LAN to an 80 ms round trip, 60 to 250 frames
per second and a release at every phase of a tick, asserting no same-tick
correction, no backward step past 0.3 mm and a stop within 0.5 mm of the
server's rest. Motion strips (strafe and stop, reversal, jump, dodge, fire)
were inspected for each after run: no step back at a stop, the dodge hop
reads, the punch is visible and the impact lands at the crosshair.
