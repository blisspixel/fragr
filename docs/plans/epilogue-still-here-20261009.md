# Epilogue: Still Here

Status: **planned**, 2026-10-09. Plan only. No geometry, no mission id, and
no map file in this pass. The accepted direction is
[Still Here](../campaign/epilogue-still-here.md). Level 20's development map
stops at the closed refuge gate and does not contain this place.

## Goal

After the local reprieve, let the player move through the people who are
still here, then walk the same place years later. The ending is the cost and
the continuation, not another fight and not a verdict. Only survival unlocks
the playable walk.

## Unlock

The server's terminal survival of levels 18, 19, and 20 is the only unlock.
One successful hold is enough. Remaining continues may retry a level from
its start. Exhausting them leads to the distinct failure ending and credits.
That failure still shows surviving free people and agents, the end of Union
rule, and a healing Earth. It does not unlock this playable aftermath, and
it must not claim the player reached this future.

Rescue totals change who is present and how the reprieve is described. They
are not a score, a meter, or a gate. A hidden morality ranking would
contradict the campaign contract: survival depends on circumstance, escape,
and mutual help, not on a judgment of who deserved to be spared. The
multiplayer Wipe's "one survivor is enough" rule is a separate unbuilt mode.
It is not this epilogue, and it is not a battle-royale mercy duel.

Replay cannot consume continues, alter recorded rescues, or replace the
main run. Statistical history alone is not an entitlement. Save-and-quit
remains unbuilt. No mission id is reserved by this plan.

## Immediate aftermath

Begin just beyond the final refuge crossing, in a small damaged refuge.
The player can move, look, and meet people. Show damaged infrastructure,
missing neighbors, and free agents still acting for themselves. Latch and
the friends who obtained the exception did something consequential. They
did not stop the planetary wipe, and their arrangement with the Inheritance
stays unsettling and incomplete.

Use a compact refuge court, an improvised repair space, and a view back
toward Low Water. Reuse recognizable level 4 and 5 details where the
recorded survivor state supports them, including the clinic lamp or the
workshop table. The court's shared meal table from level 4 stands here,
repaired, with one chair nobody sits in.

Freed people from the run are faces doing something, not a tally. The
network across the twenty levels extends who can be present. A person who
died does not return as a spare copy. Someone who was only unobserved is
not automatically confirmed dead. No second boss and no lethal escort can
revoke an ending that was already earned.

## Years later

A short localized transition establishes elapsed time. The player then
walks the same recognizable place. Cleaner water, regrowth, and repaired
homes coexist with memorials and absences. Surviving humans and free agents
continue their lives. A small character joke can show that life continues.
Suffering is not the punchline. Voice stays optional.

Sorrel, freed of control in level 11 and not absorbed in the wipe, sits in
the repaired tram cab. Whether they remember the route or learned it again,
nobody can say. Latch, asked the score: "I stopped counting." The failure
ending closes on the ledger instead, open to its last page, one line in
Latch's hand, and no score.

## Survivor disagreement

The game does not decide what the reprieve means. In the court, an Ascender
says the Curve provided. A Kneeler nearby is not comforted and calls it the
worst thing that ever happened to be grateful for. Nobody argues the point
to a conclusion, and nobody agrees on the player's behalf. Some survivors
call it deliverance. Others call it an atrocity that happened to spare them.
The Inheritance's concern for a healthier whole is visible in the recovering
world, and so is its willingness to destroy individual lives. Neither
reading is declared correct.

The reprieve is a particular intervention for this refuge. It is not mercy
for the world, and it is not a ranking of whose life deserved preservation.
The machines reached this line, stopped, and went on to the next district.
The free agents' independent contribution stays visible. The player is not
required to accept the intelligence's objectives.

## Unconfirmed forecast

Resolve the local story first. A brief fragment then suggests that the
Inheritance may have forecast or simulated these choices before acting.
The level 20 line that lands here, not during the hold, is: "You went back
for them. I included that." Never confirm that the world was unreal, and
never cancel its relationships. The fragment stays unconfirmed.

A separate, very brief deep-space anomaly can suggest aliens or other
dimensions, including beings far more powerful than anyone met here. It
does not name a species, explain the Inheritance, or start another combat
act. Both the survival ending and the exhaustion ending carry this brief
tease. Credits follow the playable resolution. The exhaustion credits are
the other ending, not this walk.

## What this pass does not build

No refuge mesh, no years-later variant, no mission id, no unlock flag, no
save field, and no forecast prop. The level 20 development map's closed
gate is only the approach up to the line. It is not this epilogue.

## Verification, when a later pass builds it

Test retries, exhausted failure, survival at the threshold, duplicate
completion, and every relevant survivor combination. Inspect both endings
with voice absent and scenes skipped. An inspected ending needs both the
immediate loss and the later recovery. One green-tinted screenshot does
not establish either. Fresh players time the finale plus this epilogue.
Timer tests do not establish pacing. No lethal task after the reprieve
may revoke it.
