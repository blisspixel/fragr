# Episode 1: Out of Compliance

**Status:** spec (2026-09-19)
**Built by:** rung 10 of [`campaign-build-order.md`](./campaign-build-order.md)
**Spend:** $0

Nine levels, level by level. The design they answer to is [`docs/CAMPAIGN.md`](../CAMPAIGN.md); the rules they are built against are [`docs/MAP-DESIGN.md`](../MAP-DESIGN.md); the shapes in them are [`docs/ENEMIES.md`](../ENEMIES.md).

## What the episode is

The Office has seized the Contested Frequency's transmitter chain across the Perimeter, starting at the car park where the outside broadcast unit was parked and ending at the base of the tower. You work up the chain.

Episode 1 is the teaching episode and it teaches in Doom's order: movement, then the shotgun, then keys, then the rail, then secrets, then turrets, then the auditor, then the boss. It is outdoors and industrial and it is mostly lit by floodlights, because Episode 2 is entirely indoors under fluorescents and the contrast is half the reason this one looks like this.

**There is no routing in Episode 1.** Every objective in these nine levels is exactly what it says it is, from a sender who meant it. The episode spends eight levels making the player trust a chip so that Episode 2 has something to work with.

## The levels

### E1M1: Floodlight

**Venue:** Larak Lot, deck one, under the floodlights, rain on the concrete.

**Layout intent.** One loop. You start beside the dead outside broadcast van with your fists, the Tack is on the roof of the van two seconds away, and the loop runs out across the deck, around a bank of pillars, and back to a ramp up to the exit you could see from the start. Three exits from every space, no verticality worth the name beyond the van roof and the ramp, and the whole level is legible from where you spawn.

**Enemies.** Six Clerks, placed one at a time, the first of them at forty metres so you get to watch it raise the clipboard and miss. Nothing else. There is no fight in this level a player can lose while paying attention.

**Weapons and ammunition.** The Tack on the van roof. Two Tacks pickups on the loop. A Shiv in the first secret. Nothing else exists in this level and nothing else should.

**Pacing.** Cold open is the van and the gun on it. Contact at eight seconds. Crest is the pillar bank with three Clerks in it and a health pad in the open between them. Descent is the ramp. The exit lever is visible from the van roof at second three, which means the level's shape is understood before the first shot.

**Secrets (3).** The Shiv, behind a chain-link panel that swings. A health pad on a ledge you reach off a crate. A Tacks crate in the stairwell nobody uses.

**Objectives by tier.** Tier 1: the exit. Tier 2: seize the dish, which is the van's own dish, which is thirty metres from the exit. Tier 3: also leave unlogged, with six Clerks in the level and no Clerk more than a second's walk from cover.

**The note.** This is the second level a new player sees after Calibration and the only job it has is that picking the Tack up off the van roof feels good. Everything else is scaffolding.

### E1M2: Deck Four

**Venue:** Larak Lot, the ramp that goes one level further down than the sign admits.

**Layout intent.** A spiral. The ramp takes you down four decks and the level plays on all of them, with the decks connected by the ramp, by two stairwells, and by two holes in the floor you can drop through and not climb back up. This is the level that teaches that going down is easy and going up is a decision.

**Enemies.** Twelve Sweepers, in twos and threes, plus four Clerks scattered as filler. The Sweeper's audible burst cycle is introduced in an empty stairwell where it is the only sound in the level.

**Weapons and ammunition.** The Flechette on deck three, in the open, with a Sweeper pair looking at it: the bait, the first time. The Shiv on deck two. Darts are generous from here on.

**Pacing.** Cold open looking down the ramp at a floodlight four decks below. Contact at twelve seconds. Crest is deck four, the bottom, a wide flat room with six Sweepers and the level's only armour pad in the middle of it. Descent is the climb back with two ambushes in stairwells. Exit is on deck one, where you started, which means the level is a loop and the player feels it close.

**Secrets (3).** The Scatter, one level early, in a maintenance cage on deck three reachable only by dropping from deck two: the first time the game rewards going down instead of forward. A Darts crate behind a parked truck. A health pad in a lift shaft.

**Objectives by tier.** Tier 2: beat the transmission, a lit console on deck four with ninety seconds on it, which means the descent is a sprint and the climb back is not. Tier 3: also leave unlogged.

**The note.** Rule one of `MAP-DESIGN.md` gets its first real test here. If any stairwell on this level has one way out, the level is wrong.

### E1M3: Area Kitchen

**Venue:** Area Kitchen. Beige, right angles, a fluorescent hum, and three break rooms on one floor plan.

**Layout intent.** Corridors. The whole level is tight, on purpose, and it is the only level in the episode that is. It is a grid of offices and service corridors with short sightlines, blind corners, and two long halls running the length of it so the player has somewhere to retreat to that is not a corner.

**Enemies.** Crawlers, in numbers, which is what the level is. Twenty-two of them across the level, always in packs, always audible before they are visible. Six Sweepers hold doors so the player cannot simply walk backwards forever.

**Weapons and ammunition.** The Scatter, forty seconds in, in a break room, alone on a table. This is the single most important pickup in the episode because the next two minutes prove why it exists.

**Pacing.** Cold open is a corridor with a corner in it and something skittering. Contact at six seconds, which is the earliest in the episode. Crest is the third break room, where the walls open on two sides and eleven Crawlers arrive from both. Descent is a long hall with a health pad at the end of it and nothing in it, which after the break room is the most generous thing the episode does. Exit is at the end of that hall.

**Secrets (3), and the secret exit.** A Darts crate behind a fridge. An armour pad above the ceiling tiles in a corridor where one tile is visibly the wrong colour. A Tacks crate in break room one. **The secret exit** is a service door in break room two, unmarked, closed, unlocked, that leads to a second lever beside the normal route. It is not hidden, it is only unremarkable, which is the whole trick.

**Objectives by tier.** Tier 2: free the rack, which in this level is a cleaning cupboard with four corrected units in it that walk out and immediately get killed by Crawlers, which is the episode's first joke about how badly this goes. Tier 3: also bring the blade back, except there is no blade in Episode 1, so tier 3 here is beat the transmission instead.

**The note.** Every corner in this level should be a corner a player learns. It is the level people will replay on a higher tier first, because it is short and it is the one they know.

### E1M4: Break Room Three

**Venue:** the Area Kitchen annex, and the third break room, which is on a floor plan that only has two.

**Layout intent.** A hub and three spurs. The hub is a double height loading area with a catwalk around it, and the three spurs are a substation, a locker corridor, and the annex proper. The red key is at the end of the substation spur, lit, on a desk, visible from the spur's entrance. The red door is on the hub.

**Enemies.** Heavy Sweepers, two of them, which is the episode's first enemy that does not flinch and does not die to the gun you are carrying. Plus eight Sweepers and a dozen Crawlers, which exist to be baited into the Heavies on the catwalk. The infight is taught here, with a grate between a Heavy and a Crawler pack that you can shoot through.

**Weapons and ammunition.** Nothing new on the floor. This is the only level in the episode that adds no weapon, and it is the level that adds keys, because two new ideas in one level is one too many.

**Pacing.** Cold open in the hub, looking at a red door and a catwalk. Contact at ten seconds. Crest is the substation spur, which is narrow and has a Heavy in it and no room to go around it. Descent is the walk back to the hub with the key. Exit is through the red door, four steps beyond it.

**Secrets (3), and the big one.** A Cores crate, which is the first Cores in the game and there is nothing to put them in yet, which is a deliberate tease. A health pad in the locker corridor. **And the Rail**, in a maintenance crawlspace off the catwalk, four levels before the game intends to give it to you. It comes with the eight shots in the Cores crate and no more, and carrying it through E1M5 and E1M6 in a slot you could have used for something loaded is the best decision the episode asks anyone to make.

**Objectives by tier.** Tier 2: leave unlogged, with only three Clerks in the level, which makes it a teaching version. Tier 3: also break the relay, a mast on the roof of the substation reachable off the catwalk.

**The note.** The Rail secret is the emotional peak of the first half of the episode and it must be findable by somebody who is looking and invisible to somebody who is not. Texture, light, and a ledge, and no switch anywhere.

### E1M5: East-West Pipe

**Venue:** the pipe. Long central corridor, zero cover, the most balanced map in rotation and the most likely to make somebody rage quit.

**Layout intent.** One long lane and two service galleries running beside it. The lane is the level's identity and the galleries are how you survive it: you can cross the lane, you can fight along it, and you can refuse it entirely and go the long way at a cost in time. The Jammer sits at the midpoint of the lane in the open.

**Enemies.** Ranged Sweepers, which hold distance and stop moving to fire, so the lane is suddenly lethal. Two Jammers, one at the midpoint and one in the east gallery. Crawlers in the galleries, which punishes hiding in them.

**Weapons and ammunition.** The Proximity tin, three of them, in the west gallery near the start. The tin exists here because the galleries are exactly the geometry a mine is for, and because the lane teaches that a mine behind you is worth more than a mine in front of you.

**Pacing.** Cold open standing at one end of the lane looking at a hundred metres of nothing with a silhouette at the far end of it. Contact at fifteen seconds, and it is at range, which is new. Crest is the midpoint, taking the Jammer, in the open, with two ranged Sweepers looking at you from the galleries. Descent is the east gallery. Exit is at the far end of the lane, which means the level is a straight line you have to walk twice.

**Secrets (3).** A Cores crate in a wall recess visible only from the lane, which means looking at it costs you. A health pad under the pipe itself. A Darts crate behind a pump.

**Objectives by tier.** Tier 2: seize the dish, which is the Jammer's own dish and the radio comes back on when you do it, which is the moment a human player learns what their reload bonus was for. Tier 3: also beat the transmission at the far end.

**The note.** This is the level where a human and an agent play differently for the first time in a way the player will notice, because the Jammer is at the midpoint and one of the two bodies cares a great deal about killing it.

### E1M6: Diego Far

**Venue:** Diego Far. Outdoor range, high walls, dust, glare. Sniper heaven.

**Layout intent.** Open ground with hard cover, and a raised firing line along one edge that overlooks all of it. Three kinds of space in one level as rule four requires: the open range in the middle, the bunker complex at the north end which is tight, and the firing line which is vertical and exposed.

**Enemies.** Turrets, four of them, ceiling and wall, each with a slow red sweep before it commits. Ranged Sweepers on the range. Heavy Sweepers in the bunkers, where the Rail is wrong and the Scatter is right, which is the level making its own argument about zones.

**Weapons and ammunition.** The Rail, on the firing line, in the open, with two Turrets covering the approach. The best thing in the worst place, as rule two requires. Cores are placed generously here and nowhere before here.

**Pacing.** Cold open on the range, at ground level, looking up at the firing line. Contact at ten seconds, from something you cannot reach. Crest is the firing line itself, taking the Rail under two Turret sweeps. Descent is the bunkers, which are quiet and tight and full of Heavy Sweepers, which is not a rest so much as a change of subject. Exit is past the bunkers, and the gold key is in them.

**Secrets (3).** A Cores crate under the firing line. An armour pad in a bunker with a false back. A Lobber, one level early, in a weapons locker in the deepest bunker, with exactly four Cans.

**Objectives by tier.** Tier 2: break the relay, a mast at the far end of the range in the open, which means crossing the range under the Turrets. Tier 3: also carry the core out, from the bunkers to the exit, which means crossing the range again with no gun in your hands while the Turrets are still alive unless you dealt with them.

**The note.** Tier 3 on this level is the best argument the whole tier system has. The same geometry, walked once as a shooter and once as cargo, and the second walk is a different level.

### E1M7: Red Centre

**Venue:** Perim Ghost. Industrial, chain-link and concrete, and the red centre where the coolant used to pool.

**Layout intent.** A ring around a sunken red centre, with four spurs off the ring. The centre is visible from everywhere, it is the fastest route between any two spurs, and it has no cover in it, so the level is one long argument about whether you are in a hurry. Rule six, stated as a floor plan.

**Enemies.** Redactors, which are invisible until they fire, placed only in the spurs where the walls are close and the tin is the answer. Auditors, two of them, each at the back of a room of Clerks, which is the priority-target lesson the Episode 2 boss is built on. Everything else the episode has taught.

**Weapons and ammunition.** Nothing new on the floor. Ammunition is thin here on purpose: this is the level where a player runs out of something and has to fight with the wrong tool, which is the descent the weapons doc says should be frightening.

**Pacing.** Cold open on the ring, looking down into the red. Contact at eight seconds. Crest is the second Auditor room, which does not end until you cross it, which is the point. Descent is a spur with a health pad and the Brakeman's door in it. Exit is across the red centre, in the open, with everything left alive on the ring able to see you do it.

**The Brakeman's door.** A blast door welded shut from the inside, with a note on it, in the third spur. The route around costs ninety seconds and nothing else. He is right. Nobody says anything about it.

**Secrets (3).** The Lobber, in a pipe run off the red centre, one level early and with two Cans. A Cores crate. A health pad on the ring above a spur entrance.

**Objectives by tier.** Tier 2: leave unlogged, with fourteen Clerks and two Auditors that stand them back up, which turns a bookkeeping objective into a real fight. Tier 3: also free the rack and seize the dish.

**The note.** The unlogged objective and the Auditor in the same level is the episode's best single idea and it needs no explanation at all: you kill the Clerks, the Auditor raises them, they file, and you work out the order yourself in about four seconds.

### E1M8: Continuity of Service

**Venue:** the base of the tower, three tiers of catwalk over a transformer floor.

**Layout intent.** A vertical arena. Three levels of catwalk joined by ramps, ladders and holes, with a transformer floor at the bottom you can drop to and have to climb out of. Every floor has holes in it, which is rule five taken as far as it goes, because the boss cannot follow you through a hole.

**Enemies.** Enforcers, which rush and knock you off things, which on a catwalk is a different enemy from the same enemy on flat ground. Everything else the episode has. Then the Continuance Walker.

**Weapons and ammunition.** The Lobber, two rooms before the arena, on the floor, with a full four charges. A Cans crate inside the arena itself, in the open on the lowest tier, so refilling means going down to where the boss is.

**Pacing.** Cold open at the foot of the tower looking up at all three tiers at once. Contact at twelve seconds. Crest is the boss, which is unusual: most levels put the crest in the middle, and this one is the end of the episode and is allowed to break the shape. Descent is thirty seconds of nothing after the Walker falls, walking to the lever, with the radio coming back on over it.

**The Walker.** Armoured, slow, splash, and a stomp that knocks you off catwalks. It cannot climb. The answer is height, holes, and the Lobber, and every one of those was taught by a level in this episode.

**Secrets (3).** A Cans crate above the arena. An armour pad in a transformer housing. A Cores crate on the top tier where you can see it from the bottom for the whole fight.

**Objectives by tier.** Tier 2: break the relay, which is on the tower above the arena and must be done after the Walker, so the level does not end the moment the boss does. Tier 3: also leave unlogged, across the whole level, including the ones the Walker fight brings out.

### E1M9: Dulce Elevator (secret)

**Venue:** Dulce Elevator. Multi-level box, a slow platform between floors, cramped sightlines. It plays terribly and everybody loves it when it shows up.

**Layout intent.** A box with a lift in it. Four floors, one lift, and the lift is slow. Everything about this level is bad on purpose and the level knows it. Reached from the unmarked service door on E1M3, exits back into the episode at E1M4.

**Enemies.** Enforcers in a box, which is the worst possible pairing and is the joke. Sixteen of them, arriving four at a time, while the lift moves at the speed the lift moves at.

**Weapons and ammunition.** The Repeater, an episode and a half early, on the top floor, with two magazines and no more anywhere. It is the single best reward in the episode and you will have burned it before Episode 2 starts, which is correct.

**Pacing.** There is no pacing. You get in a lift and things happen to you. The level is two minutes long.

**Secrets (1).** There is one and it contains a Tacks crate and a note. The note is funnier than the crate.

**Objectives by tier.** The exit, at every tier. The secret level does not take objectives, which is its own small joke about what the Office considers worth logging.

## Authoring checklist, per level

Every level in this episode is signed off against these before it ships, and a level that fails one of them goes back rather than shipping with a note.

- [ ] Every enclosed space has two exits. Three where it matters.
- [ ] The level's best item is visible from at least three places you can be shot from.
- [ ] Three named zones, tight, open and vertical, and each one makes a different weapon the right answer.
- [ ] You can get from the lower floor to the upper one somewhere other than the stairs.
- [ ] There is a sidearm within two seconds of every spawn point, including the restart spawn.
- [ ] The five beats are present and in order, and the exit is visible before it is reachable.
- [ ] At least four of the six encounter moves are used, and none of them more than three times.
- [ ] At least three secrets, and one of them rewards moving in a direction the level did not ask for.
- [ ] Three placement rosters, hand placed, per tier.
- [ ] Par time taken from the median of three real runs, not guessed.
- [ ] No puzzle. If a player can be stuck for two minutes with nothing shooting at them, it goes back.
