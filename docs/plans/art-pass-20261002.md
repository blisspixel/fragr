## Method

**Tooling (Rust, `tools/spritegen`).**
- `upload` sends a local keeper through `POST /files/generate-upload-url` and the
  presigned PUT, records the public URL in `references.jsonl` beside the raw
  outputs, and never sends the credential to storage. Uploading is free.
- `reduce --key <hex>` removes a painted chroma key everywhere by L\*a\*b\*
  distance and erodes the blended fringe before palette quantisation. The
  model returns RGB with a painted background, never alpha, so prompts ask for a
  flat magenta `#FF00FF` field, which no palette swatch or subject uses.

**Consistency.** Approve one piece, then pass it as a reference to the rest.
- Viewmodel idles are edits of the existing paid raw idles (same camera, same
  framing), asked to change only the hands, the background and any emblem. The
  first approved idle's hands are passed as a second reference to the others.
- Each fire frame is an edit of its own approved idle with that idle as the only
  reference, so the weapon cannot change shape while it shoots. Idle and fire
  are reduced from the full canvas with the same settings, so they register.
- The first approved pickup is the style reference for the other pickups;
  weapon pickups also reference their own approved viewmodel.
- Reduction: palette-locked to `docs/palette.json` in L\*a\*b\*, hard alpha, no
  error diffusion. The PNG writer emits only image chunks, so no generator or
  content-credential metadata survives.

**Local, free.** The scope overlay is a GDScript bake (crisp circular aperture,
reticle, palette colours). The Ranged Sweeper is a separate rig and bake beside
the Crawler, Jammer and Notary, reusing the Sweeper body, the shared 55-pose
layout, eight directions and feet registration; existing atlases are untouched.

## Integration

- `client/scripts/weapon_art.gd` (`WeaponArt`) is the one table of weapon and
  supply pictures, keyed by wire name. The HUD, the held fighter weapon and the
  pickup presenter read it; the old 32 pixel icons and the 2026-09-19
  viewmodels it replaced are removed.
- HUD: a shot swaps to the gun's fire frame for 0.08 s and the Shotgun shows its
  pump from 0.18 to 0.44 s; the translation kick still applies, and the generic
  muzzle star is kept only for a gun without a drawn fire frame. A grenade that
  the local fighter actually threw, seen in an authoritative snapshot, raises the
  off hand for 0.34 s while the gun dips. Health and armour gain icons at twice
  their 20 pixel size; the numbers keep their size.
- Pickups: one texel density (1/72 m), fixed-Y billboards, unshaded, a gentle
  bob, resting on the floor. No world text except the golden Railgun's name; the
  labelled crate remains the fallback for a kind without art.
- Fighters hold the same side profiles at one density, so a pistol is smaller
  than a railgun.

## Level 7 contract

Art only; Level 7 owns rules, wire names and placement.

- **Sniper Rifle.** First-person `res://assets/weapons/viewmodels/sniper_idle.png`
  and `sniper_fire.png` (241 by 180, full canvas, same registration as the other
  guns); pickup and held profile `res://assets/weapons/pickups/sniper.png`
  (100 by 20). `WeaponArt` keys them as `"Sniper"`; if the wire name differs,
  change only that key. The fire frame's flash is ember with a white core, so
  it reads apart from the Railgun's cyan; an ember tracer would match it.
- **Scope overlay.** `res://assets/weapons/sniper/scope_overlay.png`, 360 by 360,
  `WeaponArt.SCOPE_OVERLAY`. Draw it centred at the full window height with
  nearest sampling and fill the remaining width with ink (`#0A0A0C`); hide the
  viewmodel and crosshair while scoped. The aperture is transparent with a
  dithered edge and a red chevron at the aim point.
- **Ranged Sweeper.** Atlas `res://assets/characters/union/ranged_sweeper.png` in
  the shared `EnemyAnimation` layout (55 poses, 8 directions, 160 pixel cells,
  feet registered with the Sweeper). Add `"ranged_sweeper"` to
  `ActorState.KINDS` and send that kind; `EnemyView` loads the atlas by name and
  the Union shader keeps the glint full-bright. The windup (`raise`) grows the
  glint over its four frames and holds the last, so the server's windup phase is
  the tell; the first fire pose has a muzzle flash.
