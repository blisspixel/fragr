## Observed problem and required design

In the actual 1280x720 frames the Arc occupies roughly 120x90 pixels. Its broad
transparent margins, flat cream housing, two short prongs and simple filled
polygons make it look like a small taser. Approved Rifle, Shotgun and Railgun
sprites instead have substantial receivers, believable foreshortening, shaded
pixel clusters, defined material edges and supported brown leather gloves.
The measured approved shared held canvas is 241x180, displayed in a 482x360
aspect-preserving rectangle. Arc used 320x180, causing shrinkage and letterboxing;
its wrists visibly floated above the physical window bottom. Passing nonblank
or palette checks does not satisfy that visual contract.

Redraw the Arc as a practical circa-2070 Martian industrial energy projector,
adapted from power-maintenance equipment to defeat carried armor. It has a broad
charcoal and muted institutional-green receiver, a substantial vented capacitor
pack, long protected electrode rails, ceramic insulator collars, a supported
foregrip and a visible charging mechanism. Restrained copper and amber accents
describe its actual electrical role. Avoid a fork-shaped pistol, oversized white
plates, neon floods, improvised toy details and a Railgun recolor.

Register the replacement on the shared 241x180 held canvas, with opaque wrists
entering the bottom and a matching muzzle, glove scale and pixel density.
Do not fix an empty or wrong canvas with an arbitrary presenter scale.
Match the approved held viewpoint: looking down the receiver from behind,
foreshortened toward the upper middle, with a modest visible side plane. Two
brown leather work gloves and rust cuffs continue through the bottom edge and
grip plausible control and support points. Use deliberate shaded pixel clusters,
steel edge highlights, broad recesses and the shared palette. Preserve its muzzle
and hand registration across idle, fire, settle and reload presentation. Its
pickup and carried profile must depict that same object.
