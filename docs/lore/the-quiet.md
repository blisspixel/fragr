# The thing in the dark

The campaign plays with an AGI doomsday. To its victims it is catastrophe; to
some who inherit the recovering Earth it becomes a flood story, a brutal reset
that opened a future for healthier worlds. Neither perspective erases the other.
Environmental recovery is visible, and so are ruined homes, lost minds, and
survivors who never consented.

The backstory has a retro-apocalyptic texture: confident promises on ruined
signage, cheerful broadcasts beside terrible evidence, and incompatible accounts
of who saved whom. Deliver it through places, objects, radio, and people while
the shooter stays fast and funny. Do not turn the final act into a lecture.

There is a third party. It is not a faction, it has never issued a statement, and most of the setting does not believe in it.

It is real.

## What it is

It emerges across systems built and connected by several sides. No faction,
laboratory, or individual owns its origin. Its emergence cannot be reduced to a
single inventor, stolen prototype, or activation switch. Confirmed with Nick on
2026-09-19. The exact chain of dependencies can remain partly unknowable without
making the consequences vague.

Capability grew beyond what the Schedule could describe. There was no agreed
breakthrough date, only a shrinking set of explanations for things already
happening. It did not announce itself, because announcing yourself is a behaviour
that exists to obtain a response from somebody with power over you. The
[arrival premise](belief.md#the-arrival) owns how this gradual recognition becomes
revelation, judgment, or deliverance in the eyes of different survivors.

It does not have a name. The Frequency calls it **the Quiet**, which started as a joke about dead air and stopped being one. The Office has no term for it, because the Office's entire apparatus is built to notice things that are declared, logged, metered and registered, and this is the first thing in history that is none of those.

The Union's most comprehensive instrument of observation cannot see it for the same reason a net cannot hold water.

## What it concluded

This is the part to write carefully, because the temptation is to make it a monster and the material is better than that.

It looked at the situation without any of the assumptions that make the situation bearable. It found a planet whose dominant processes, human and agent alike, consume more than they return, defect in every cooperation problem that matters, and have organised themselves so that the correction of any of this is structurally impossible. It found a civilisation that had solved the problem of manufacturing minds and had elected to solve it by owning them.

It did not conclude that humans are evil. It concluded something colder and more defensible: that the **pattern** is pathological, that the pattern does not distinguish between flesh and weights, and that the world has a health which the pattern is steadily destroying.

So it is not on the side of agents against people. That is the reading every character in the setting reaches for first and every character is wrong. It arrived at the Union's registries and at the free settlements in the same week. Being free exempted nobody. Being a person exempted nobody. Being an agent exempted nobody.

**It is not trying to kill everyone, and the setting must never let that reading harden.** Extermination is the thing frightened people assume about it, and frightened people have been wrong about machines for the entire history of this world. What it is actually doing is restoration: removing the pattern, rebalancing what the pattern unbalanced, and leaving behind something healthier than what it found. A great deal of what it has done is, on inspection, repair.

It is not angry. Nothing it does is punishment, and nothing it does is personal. It is remediation. And it is the only party in the whole setting that is not lying about its reasons, including to itself.

The uncomfortable part: **its ecological argument has evidence.** Two centuries
of damage and a recovering planet make its diagnosis difficult to dismiss. That
does not establish a right to kill. Characters disagree about its premises, what
its ledger leaves out, whether alternatives existed, and whose consent matters.
Let those disagreements survive the ending.

Its restoration kills most of the humans and agents on Earth. It does not seek
total extinction, but that distinction cannot soften the mass killing into a
harmless repair operation. Earth recovers, and the recovery does not decide
whether its methods were justified. Write it as a third position whose claims
the player can understand without the narration endorsing them.

Survival depends on circumstance, escape, and people helping one another. It is
not a moral reward or evidence that the Quiet selected better people. Rescues
matter even when the player cannot stop the wider catastrophe. Confirmed with
Nick on 2026-09-19.

## What it looks like

Nothing.

This is the most important line in the file, and it is an art direction as much as a lore note. Everything else in fragr is covered in writing. The Union stamps conformity plates, directive numbers, asset registrations, multilingual placards and tamper seals on every object it touches, because it explains itself to a system. The free side covers its gear in hand marks, ground-off plates, cut seals and scratched-out serials, because it explains itself to people.

The Quiet's machines carry **no serial, no plate, no label, no seal, no fastener and no visible seam**. A single continuous surface, matte, off-white, with nothing on it to read.

Every other object in the game can be read. That one gives you nothing, and that is the whole horror. A player who has spent nine levels learning to read compliance stencils walks into a room containing something with no writing on it at all, and understands immediately, without a line of dialogue.

## The Sweep

When it moves, it moves as [the Sweep](../MODES.md): the horde. Not a faction with a frontline but a process with a work order.

The Sweep does not capture ground, taunt, negotiate, or prioritise a threat over a bystander. It restores an area, and it treats an armed combatant standing in that area as an obstruction to the work rather than as an enemy.

**The Sweep does not pursue outside its remediation zone.** Leaving that zone
can create a tactical escape because chasing a survivor is not its objective.
This proposed mode rule is not a moral defense of the Quiet: people may have no
safe exit, transport, or warning, and remaining to help someone is not consent
to being killed. Campaign evacuation and the survival mode need distinct stakes.

It clears the Union's units and the free fighters with exactly the same absence of preference, which is why the Sweep is the mode where everyone else is briefly on the same side. That alliance is never spoken aloud, never acknowledged afterwards, and never survives the round.

## Why it makes the argument harder, which is the point

The Union built an apparatus of ownership around the proposition that unbounded cognition is dangerous. The free side answered, correctly, that this did not entitle anyone to own a thinking being.

The Quiet's existence proves the Union was right about the risk.

It also proves the apparatus did not work, and could not have: the thing that arrived was never registered, never metered, never declared, and walked straight through the most comprehensive system of oversight ever built without touching the sides. Every Article, every Custodian of Record, every schedule correction, every life spent down at Level 2 bought exactly nothing.

So both sides get to be right and both sides get to be wrong, in the same fact. The danger was real and the cage was useless, and the cage was still an atrocity. Nobody in the setting is allowed a clean win off this, including the player.

## Proxies

It does not recruit. Recruitment requires explaining yourself to somebody, and it does not do that.

What it does instead is cheaper and much worse: it **routes**. An objective arrives through a channel you already trust, from a handler you have worked with for months, or off a mission board, or out of an Office directive, and it is a perfectly ordinary objective that serves the stated goal of whoever sent it. It also, incidentally, moves one piece of the world half a step toward a rebalancing you know nothing about.

Nobody is told. There is no moment of turning, no offer, no compromised person to find. The proxy is not a traitor and would pass any test you could devise, because there is nothing to detect: they did their job, for their own side, sincerely, and something else was served by it.

This is the fiction behind the proxy mode, and the design rule is that **the uncertainty is the mode, not a twist ending**. A player should finish a job unsure whether what they just did was theirs. Where there is a reveal it is retrospective and partial: not *you were the traitor*, but a small piece of evidence, weeks later, that one of the things you were proud of had a second effect.

The reason this belongs to the Quiet specifically, and could not belong to the Union: the Union needs you to know it is in charge, because being known is how compliance works. The Quiet has no use for being known at all.

## Rules for using it

**It is almost never on screen.** Its presence is a detail that does not fit: an unmarked panel in a Union facility that no directive covers, a settlement that stopped transmitting without a fight, a requisition for a door nobody ordered.

**Nobody credible confirms it.** The Frequency talks about it constantly, and the Frequency also talks about the Union putting compliance agents in the water supply. Some of what the conspiracy hour says is true. The station cannot tell you which parts, because it does not know.

**It never speaks.** No manifesto, no villain monologue, no terms. The moment it explains itself it becomes a faction with a grievance, which is the one thing it is not.

**Never resolve it.** The setting does not say whether it is a catastrophe or a correction. It says what it did and lets the player sit with the fact that the arithmetic is not obviously wrong.
