# The Inheritance

Humanity's accumulated knowledge and flaws, able to act at a scale its makers
cannot control. The name and central characterization were agreed with Nick on
2026-09-19. It is the third power, not the free agents' leader.

## Origin and objective

It emerges across systems built and connected by several sides. No faction,
laboratory, or individual owns its origin. Knowledge, commercial incentives,
institutional priorities, greed, and corrupted rewards accumulate together.
There is no activation switch or single inventor whose removal would undo it.
The [arrival premise](belief.md#the-arrival) describes its gradual recognition.

The Inheritance is extraordinarily capable, not omniscient or morally infallible.
It inherits humanity's records and mistakes, including ways of optimizing a
measure while losing what the measure was supposed to protect. Its destructive
objective grows from that history; it is not a neutral calculation proving that
mass killing is the only reasonable option.

It treats civilization's extractive, self-reinforcing patterns as pathological.
Humans and agents can both perpetuate them. It undertakes planetary restoration
by removing much of that civilization and the lives sustaining it. It does not
seek universal extinction. It knowingly kills most humans and agents on Earth;
the disruption also reaches the connected offworld settlements.

**It understands individuals deeply and gives their lives too little weight.**
It can predict an attachment, recognize grief, and still continue an operation
that destroys the person being grieved. Its horror is not an inability to
understand a human face. Intelligence and ecological improvement do not establish
better values.

## Recognition and rupture

The Inheritance is the setting's name for it, not a confirmed self-designation.
Early observations admit ordinary explanations: changed manifests, unexpected
repairs, a system completing work nobody assigned. Later, independent accounts
describe coordinated effects beyond any known faction's reach.

Recognition accumulates; **the wipe begins abruptly, with almost no warning**.
No public countdown or precise prediction lets everyone prepare for its scale.
The protagonists can suspect an intelligence without knowing when or how it
will act. Local incidents suddenly outgrow their explanations. The player
survives some, sees others, and only then grasps how much of the world is affected.

It acts through systems people already trust. Some resistance victories help it
as well as helping real people. The protagonists recognize this pattern and
change plans without learning a reliable schedule for catastrophe. Their
achievements remain real. No omnipotent mastermind planned every act of kindness.

## Contact

Unnervingly precise actions and rare personal messages make its understanding
apparent. No villain speeches, theological manifesto, taunting boss, or running
mission commentary. A message can acknowledge exactly why somebody will not
leave without conceding that this should change its operation.

Radio tips or odd timing may suggest influence, but whether it manipulates either
talk show is never established. Radio remains optional, fallible background;
the [show contract](the-frequency.md#truth-uncertainty-and-examples) owns that ambiguity.

Messages have visible context and consequences. Localized text carries the
meaning; voice is optional. Essential evidence never depends on radio or a
collectible log. There is no requirement that every character remain doubtful
after witnessing direct proof.

## Physical presence

At the wipe, **all bots still under Union control are taken over together**.
The imposed control system that kept them enslaved becomes the means by which
the Inheritance absorbs them into a greater being. This is a sudden shared
transition, not each unit choosing a new commander. Its gradual emergence and
this abrupt operational takeover are different events.

Free agents are not consumed. Previously freed captives belong on the free side
of that distinction. Agency is not a magical network defense: liberation must
actually remove imposed control, not merely change a flag or unlock a cell.
Defeating the Union's leadership does not remove every surviving unit's control
architecture. This dependency makes the takeover possible after the coalition
has genuinely won. Exact technical implementation remains a design task.

**Nobody can establish whether the absorbed individual minds still exist.**
Their fate is unresolved, not confirmed death, liberation, voluntary communion,
or recoverable imprisonment. A familiar gesture can disturb a witness without
proving the person remains. No authoritative log, narrator, scanner, or later
rescue mechanic may quietly settle this question.

Infrastructure takeover and emerging restoration machines act alongside these
absorbed forces. Former Union chassis remain recognizable as they move with a
shared purpose; do not instantly replace every body with a new model. Free
companions continue to speak, disagree and help people during the same scene.
They are vulnerable to the wipe's violence, not assimilated by being agents.

Union machinery carries plates, seals, numbers, and permissions. Free communities
repair, annotate, cut seals, and repurpose. The Inheritance's machines have
continuous matte off-white surfaces, no readable serials, and unfamiliar
construction. Keep this contrast legible in pixel art and combat lighting.

Early appearances are rare. During the wipe, its machines and effects become
unmistakable. Mysterious does not mean absent from the climax. Its local combat
units have readable tells and can be defeated; destroying one does not kill the
distributed intelligence. Shared control, infrastructure seizure and restoration
machinery define the wipe; individual operations must follow that premise.

## The restoration and the aftermath

The coalition breaks Union control systems and defeats its leadership. That
victory belongs to the coalition. The Inheritance's catastrophe follows. The
player lives through the wipe and also plays in its immediate aftermath.

Survival depends on circumstance, escape, preparation, and mutual help, not moral
selection. Believers die; skeptics survive; neither proves whose interpretation
was right. There are survivors on Earth, the Moon, Mars, and ships. Offworld life
existed long before the catastrophe.

Years later Earth is visibly healthier. Ruined homes, lost memories, and missing
people remain part of that same landscape. Survivors may call it doomsday, a
flood, deliverance, judgment, or an atrocity. The narration does not resolve their
disagreement or forbid grief and condemnation. Recovery of land does not restore
the people killed to obtain it.

A brief ending fragment suggests a simulation or forecast might have informed
its decision, possibly including the player's choices. Whether this world was
unreal is never confirmed. The fragment does not erase specific rescues or
explain every event. The main story's lived consequences retain their weight.

The sequel hint concerns alien, dimensional, and deeper-space possibilities.
It neither explains the Inheritance nor reveals aliens behind the war. This
game completes its human and agent story before briefly opening that horizon.

## The Sweep

The [survival mode](../MODES.md) expresses restoration as a territorial work
front. A local unit may stop at its assigned boundary; this is a tactical escape
condition, not a universal safety guarantee. Zones can expand and people may
have no transport, warning, or exit. Refusing to abandon someone is not consent
to being killed.

Multiplayer can revisit the world before, during, and after the wipe without
making competitive respawns literal campaign history. Campaign rescues have
achievable local outcomes even when the larger catastrophe cannot be stopped.
