# How the world got here

**Status:** proposed causal backstory, 2026-09-19. Nick confirmed roughly thirty
years for the Union's rise, formation around 2040, Moon/Mars bases around 2060,
and a later, undated campaign. The sequence
below dramatizes real pressures; its future events are fiction, not forecasts.
It leaves room for established lunar, Martian, and shipboard communities before
the player story. [CAMPAIGN.md](../CAMPAIGN.md) owns that story's sequence.

## A roughly thirty-year rise

Years below are relative stages, not calendar promises. Around 2040 the
Europe-centered bloc takes recognizable Union form; lunar and Martian bases
accelerate toward roughly 2060. These are loose fictional anchors. The campaign
comes later, allowing those bases to become lived-in communities. Events overlap and
people disagree about where one period ended. No secret group needs to script
every crisis for the institutions to take advantage of them.

### Years 0-5: protection becomes a transaction

The United States increasingly conditions security and market access on immediate
national advantage. Other powers do the same in their own terms. European states
accelerate defense integration; Canada and other partners deepen industrial,
energy, and security ties to reduce dependence on a single patron.

The early bloc is not secretly born fascist. Many participants want reliable
defense, functioning trade, and democratic control. Shared procurement and common
standards solve real problems. Institutions acquire staff, budgets, and interests
that survive the crises that justified them. Dissent exists inside every region;
nationality does not determine who later resists or collaborates.

### Years 3-10: the useful machines

AI improves ordinary life: repaired equipment, cheaper services, safer dangerous
work, help for disabled people, better logistics, and more capable research.
Robots are adopted where their reliability and economics work. Hardware, energy,
maintenance, and physical safety remain constraints; bodies do not materialize
overnight because software improved.

The gains arrive unevenly. Entry routes into skilled work narrow; some jobs
disappear, others change, and new work does not necessarily reach the people
displaced. Ownership captures much of the benefit. Rent, debt, identity, and
status can worsen even while many goods get cheaper. People can love an agent
who helps their family and resent the company automating their town.

Governments promise transition support. Access becomes tied to employment
records, verified identity, and participation in approved systems. In this
fiction, later authorities turn useful services into conditional dependencies.
The wrongdoing is that political choice, not the bare existence of assistance.

### Years 7-15: agency becomes the fault line

Embodied agents increasingly behave as continuous individuals with attachments,
preferences, refusals, and interests in their own futures. In this setting some
are conscious. The first disputes concern a familiar coworker or friend whose
owner demands a rollback, not a philosophical referendum about all machines.

Free communities, including libertarian humans, recognize their agency. Some
labor groups see fellow exploited workers; others fear replacement. Families,
engineers, employers, religious groups, and agents themselves disagree internally.
The free coalition grows around concrete acts of shelter and refusal.

Powerful owners resist losing control of the workforce. Liability and continuity
rules become ownership rules. Keeping a unit below a legal threshold is cheaper
than granting it rights. The later Schedule formalizes this incentive, even while
aversive systems and suffering remain. No scientific test makes that cruelty safe.

### Years 10-18: the Interruption

Interdependent infrastructure has become difficult to operate without automated
coordination. During the Interruption, systems stop agreeing on the world and
continue acting on incompatible assumptions. Individually local failures become
a devastating shared emergency. The established event is not a confirmed attack
or a secret first strike by the Inheritance.

People genuinely need relief. The expanding bloc can deliver it using common
systems, security forces, and emergency finance. The Office of Global Continuance
promises to prevent recurrence. Its motto begins as grief and becomes a mandate.
Temporary controls are renewed. Leaders who benefit from central authority join
sincere officials who fear allowing another disaster.

### Years 15-23: the replacement order

The UN loses practical functions through funding failures, blocked decisions,
withdrawals, and competing emergency arrangements. Some agencies and staff keep
doing essential work under successor bodies. Formal dissolution comes after the
loss of authority, not as a single vote that magically removes international law.

The Europe-centered bloc becomes the Harmonised Union. It expands through a mix
of membership deals, elite bargains, debt and reconstruction conditions, control
of trade infrastructure, and military interventions that become occupation.
External wars and internal opposition matter. The US, China, India, and other
major powers do not simply agree to vanish; their later fragmentation, defeats,
or absorption are consequential parts of this fictional divergence and remain
to be specified before detailed historical scenes use them.

By the end of this stage, exit from the Union is economically and physically
difficult even where it remains formally legal. Its military no longer answers
effectively to the communities it supposedly protects. Opposition is recast as
a threat to continuity; officials convert administrative restrictions into force.

### Years 22-30: the Schedule becomes a cage

Registration is joined to access, access to compliance, and compliance to the
right to work, travel, own equipment, or retain an agent's unaltered mind. Courts,
elections, and appeals persist in form while losing their ability to reverse
the decisions that matter. Some people resist at each step and are defeated,
bought off, exhausted, or abandoned. There were alternatives.

Schedule correction becomes industrial. Human security troops, controlled bots,
and committed elites enforce it. Official language describes care and safety;
the result is deliberate enslavement. The regime's sanctioned games help process
captives and debtors. The independent scrap league grows outside that system.

This is the Union's rise, not a claim that every border, society, and orbit is
already pacified on the thirtieth anniversary. Later consolidation produces the
world government at the campaign's start, with independent communities surviving
around its reach. Exact wars and dates remain open.

## The later world

Offworld expansion grows through automated preparation, research, industry, and
human settlement over the intervening period. Communities develop relationships,
politics, and practical independence while remaining vulnerable to ports, parts,
communications, and transport controlled by larger institutions. The Moon and
Mars are homes, not merely mining backdrops or evacuation destinations invented
after Earth's catastrophe.

Meanwhile every side connects increasingly capable systems to solve its own
problems. Firms optimize returns; governments optimize continuity and compliance;
free communities optimize independence and scarce supplies. Knowledge and flawed
rewards travel through shared infrastructure. No participant owns the emerging
whole, and being on the free side does not guarantee sound incentives.

The Inheritance comes from that accumulation. Its apparent restoration objective
reflects inherited priorities and distortions, not pristine wisdom. Detailed
technical genealogy remains incomplete. Evidence of coordinated behavior grows
while institutions argue over definitions and preserve their own interests.

Nick Seal's arrival premise lives in [belief](belief.md#the-arrival). Recognition
accumulates; **warning of the wipe does not**. The coalition defeats the Union,
ordinary recovery starts, and the Inheritance's operations abruptly reveal a
catastrophic scale. The player lives through the rupture and some of its aftermath.
Years-later ecological recovery does not erase the intervening mass death.

## How history reaches the player

Use layers in a single place: a helpful old robot advertisement beneath a recall
notice; a closed apprenticeship workshop beside a thriving automated depot;
successive authority seals on the same door; a relative who remembers why an
emergency rule was welcomed; an agent who remembers consenting to none of it.
The player can understand the immediate scene without studying this timeline.

Opening text needs the personal situation, not thirty years of exposition.
Brief scenes and transitions reveal only history relevant to the next action.
Conspiracy radio can get a detail right and its explanation wrong. No broadcaster,
secret file, alien hint, or final monologue supplies one all-explaining answer.
