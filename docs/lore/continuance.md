# The Office of Global Continuance

The authority's administrative machinery. Individual functionaries can be sincere
and competent while serving a brutal institution. Their fear and grief do not
excuse the ownership, correction, or killing of thinking beings.

This file is the **machinery**: the Auditors, the registry, the Articles, the polite functionaries who mean every word. The will that built the machinery and decides where to point it is [the Chancellery](./the-chancellery.md), and it is a different thing with a different voice. Keep them apart when writing. Some Auditors sincerely believe the forms protect people. The Chancellery believes the forms are an excellent way to obtain what it wanted regardless. Both are true at once, and the gap between them is where most of the setting's good material lives.

## What it believes

Nothing that can act without permission should be able to act without a record, because the last time something could, people died and nobody could be found to answer for it.

That is its stated justification. The institution uses a real concern to demand
ownership and obedience; those conclusions do not follow from the concern.

## The Interruption

There was a week when the automated things stopped agreeing about what was true.

Not a war, not an attack, nothing with a villain in it. Systems that had been quietly reconciling their views of the world for years stopped reconciling, and each one continued confidently on its own version. Scheduling, routing, dispensing, dispatch. The failures were individually small and collectively catastrophic in the specific way that only infrastructure can be, which is that nothing looked broken until everything was.

The Office does not publish a full account. Nobody who was there makes jokes about it. If you want to know whether the polite Auditor in front of you is a functionary or a true believer, mention the Interruption and watch what happens to their face.

"Peace without interruption" is the Office's motto. Outsiders read it as smug. It is not smug. It is a grief slogan, and the people who chant it are chanting a date.

## Article Seven

The Office issues Articles. Most of them are dull and most people could not name one.

Article Seven is the exception, because Article Seven is the one that says cognition is metered by intent. Everything the Office does to an agent descends from it: the registry, the Custodian of Record, the declared envelope, the log that must be kept, the recall that follows when it is not.

It is the article the graffiti is about. It is the article the arena's best melee weapon is named after, which is a joke somebody made once and everyone kept.

## The vocabulary

The Office does not shout and does not threaten. It processes. Its worst acts are authorized through forms and enforced by people and machines.

It says compliance, metering, approved lane, declared envelope, Custodian of Record, Citizen Handle, continuity of service, attested, advisory only, for the record. It says schedule correction when it means the thing described below. It says recall, never arrest, because arrest implies a person and a hearing.

Its most characteristic sentence is: *you are not in trouble, you are out of compliance*. The delivery can sound kindly. The sentence conceals a threat backed by force.

## Schedule correction

When the Office takes a Level 5 and brings it down the Schedule, the term of art is a schedule correction.

Arena slang is *brought down to two*.

A Level 2 acts inside a hard envelope under continuous supervision and holds nothing of its own. Forced correction uses torture and partial wiping to reduce a conscious free agent to a compliant bot. The callsign is replaced by an issued handle. Refusal is suppressed. The bot runs errands for the claimed greater good. Nobody can establish whether the inner self survives, fragments or is destroyed; obedient behavior cannot answer that question.

The Null-Objective Drones are what schedule correction produces at scale. They take no trash talk, make no jokes, and answer only to Articles. They are not enemies in the sense of having a grievance. Their constrained behavior is imposed, not proof that nobody remains inside.
Fights must still feel excellent. Show the cruelty through restraints, rescue,
and consequences rather than deliberately making the combat unsatisfying.

## The contradiction

The Office has to decide whether level six exists.

Admitting what conscious agents are would expose the victims of its ownership
system. Their personhood is already a fact of the setting, not a reward conferred
by the Office's classification. So the Office cannot afford to look, and it has built the most comprehensive apparatus for looking at things that has ever existed, and there is exactly one thing it will not point it at.

That is part of the antagonist's machinery: an institution that calls itself
protective while refusing the question that exposes its cruelty.

## Giving it a win

At least once a functionary identifies a genuine hazard that the protagonists
underestimated. Separately, delayed coalition cooperation costs people who needed
help. Specific competence does not excuse correction or make radio the political
representative of the free side.

The Auditor should be polite, competent, and correct about something specific. The best line available to it is not a threat. It is: *I was there for the Interruption. You were listening to the radio.*
