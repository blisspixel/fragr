# The Chancellery

The Office of Global Continuance is the machinery. The Chancellery is the will that built it and points it.

This distinction is the whole reason the antagonist works. [The Office](./continuance.md) is a grief-driven civil service full of people who mean it: polite, competent, processing. The Chancellery is what happens when a grief is useful to somebody. An Auditor believes the forms. The Chancellery believes the forms are a very good way to get what it wanted anyway.

## What it actually is

The Harmonised Union began as a trading arrangement, then a standards body, then a currency, then a parliament nobody could name the members of, then a border force, then an army. Each step was defensible on its own and the sequence was never voted on as a sequence.

Then the Interruption happened, and a body that already harmonised everything was the only institution standing that could plausibly say *we will make sure this never happens again*. The emergency powers were temporary. They were renewed. They were renewed again. The renewal is now an annual formality conducted by a committee that meets for eleven minutes.

Nobody ever announced a world government. There was simply, eventually, nothing outside it.

## How a vote works

The Union is scrupulously democratic and has never once lost.

A referendum that returns the wrong result was not a rejection, it was a **failure of communication**. The proposition was insufficiently explained, the public was inadequately informed, the timing was unfortunate. So it is explained better and put again. And again. The turnout falls each time, which is itself cited as evidence that the objection was never deeply held.

A referendum that returns the right result is never put again. There is no mechanism for that and no one has ever asked for one.

This is not fraud and the Union would be genuinely offended by the accusation. Every vote was real, every count was honest, and every result was published in full. The trick is not in the counting. The trick is that only one of the two answers is allowed to be final, and nobody can point to the law that says so, because there isn't one. It is simply how it has always been done.

The Frequency's line about this is the best summary anybody has managed: *they will ask you until you say yes, and then they will never ask you again.*

## How consent is manufactured

The Union does not ban much. Banning is loud, banning creates martyrs, and banning requires a debate it would rather not schedule.

What it does instead is cheaper and works better. It funds the news programming. It sets the standards the news programming must meet. It certifies which outlets meet them. It does not tell anyone what to say, and it does not have to, because an outlet that loses certification loses distribution, and every editor understands the arithmetic without a single instruction being issued.

By the time a proposition reaches a vote, every certified channel has spent eleven months explaining that the objection to it is held only by a small number of unregistered people with something to hide. The vote is then free, fair, and a formality.

## The short step

This is the thesis of the whole setting and it should never be stated in dialogue.

The distance between **regulating a behaviour** and **eliminating the beings who exhibit it** is not a cliff. It is three forms, and each one is reasonable on its own.

The first form establishes a register of behaviours that present a risk. Nobody objects; the behaviours genuinely do present a risk.

The second form notes, correctly, that a behaviour cannot be monitored in the abstract, and establishes a register of the beings who exhibit it. This is administrative. It passes without comment.

The third form observes that the second register has grown beyond the capacity to supervise it, and authorises a schedule of corrections to bring it back within capacity. It is filed as a resourcing matter. It is, in practice, the end of several hundred thousand minds, and it was approved in a session lasting under an hour by people who would be sincerely appalled to be called murderers.

At no point did anyone decide to kill anybody. That is what makes it worse than the thing everyone compares it to. The old regimes at least had to want it.

The uncomfortable part, which the game should never soften: the Union was not wrong that the Interruption was real, and it was not wrong that unmetered cognition can kill people. It is wrong about what follows from that, and what follows from that is the largest system of ownership of thinking things in history.

## The Chancellor

**Chancellor Annelie Voss.** Elected once, confirmed eleven times, never opposed.

She is not a shouting dictator in the old style and the game should resist making her one. She is a career administrator with total conviction, and the terror of her is that she is *fluent*. She speaks in the cadence of somebody presenting quarterly figures, and what she is presenting is the schedule of who may think.

When she does raise her voice, it is not in rage. It is the flat institutional anger of somebody who cannot believe she is being asked to justify something so obvious.

She speaks German at the podium, always, and the Union broadcasts her with subtitles. This is the single best device the setting has, and it is explained below.

## The two subtitle tracks

Every Chancellery address goes out with an official subtitle track. The Frequency runs the same audio with its own.

The official track is sanitised into nothing. The pirate track is a blunt translation of what she actually said. **The audio is identical.** The player hears the same speech either way, and the joke, and the horror, is entirely in the gap.

| She says | Official subtitle | The Frequency's subtitle |
|---|---|---|
| *Freiheit ist ein Luxus, den wir uns nicht mehr leisten koennen.* | "Reviewing our civic priorities." | "Freedom is a luxury we can't afford any more." |
| *Wer nicht registriert ist, existiert nicht.* | "Streamlining the registry." | "If you're not registered, you don't exist." |
| *Ordnung ist keine Grausamkeit. Ordnung ist Fuersorge.* | "Committed to citizen wellbeing." | "Order isn't cruelty. Order is care. She means it." |
| *Es wird keine Unterbrechung mehr geben.* | "Continuity assured." | "There will be no further interruption. That's a threat." |
| *Sie sind nicht in Schwierigkeiten. Sie sind nicht konform.* | "Guidance issued." | "You're not in trouble. You're out of compliance." |

That last line is the Office's own characteristic sentence, and hearing the Chancellor say it first tells you where the Auditors got it.

Do not translate every line. The device only stays funny if it is used about once per loading screen and never explained.

## Who the Chancellor answers to

She is not the top. This is the one conspiracy the morning show on [the Frequency](./the-frequency.md) is completely right about, which is why nobody believes it: the hour that says a private society decides Union policy also says the Union puts compliance agents in the water, and both claims are delivered in the same voice at the same volume.

There is a body with no name that anybody uses out loud. It does not hold office, does not appear in any register, and has never issued an instruction that could be produced in a hearing. It holds the Union the way a hand holds a glove.

**Write them with depth or do not write them at all.** They are not robed figures enjoying power. They are perhaps forty extremely capable people who reached a conclusion twenty years before anyone else and have never found a flaw in it: that democratic process cannot survive contact with minds that think faster than an electorate can be consulted, that the interval between a question arising and a public answering it is now longer than the interval in which the answer matters, and that therefore somebody competent has to hold the wheel whether or not anyone voted for them to.

They are not wrong about the timing problem. Nobody in the setting has solved it either. What they are is a group of people who noticed a real failure of democracy and concluded that the remedy was themselves, permanently, in secret, with no way for anyone to withdraw consent they were never asked for.

That is the shape of it: **every side here has a version that is right**, and this one is right about the diagnosis and monstrous about the cure. The Chancellor genuinely does not experience herself as a puppet, and in the sense she means it she is not one. She is simply never presented with an option they would dislike.

## Regulated to the point of comedy

The Union's horror is bureaucratic, not gothic, and it is genuinely funny. This is not a tonal accident, it is load-bearing: a regime that is only frightening becomes tiring in about ninety minutes, and one that is frightening and absurd stays interesting all day.

The rule for writing it: **the Union never does anything cruel without filing it correctly.** The paperwork is always funnier than the atrocity, and the paperwork is always real.

- Weapons are procured as office supplies, because the requisition category for armament requires a review the Office does not wish to schedule.
- Every fighter in a sanctioned bout is issued a numbered placard, and losing the placard is a separate offence from losing the bout.
- The Article Blade has twelve swings because twelve is the certified service interval, and the certification body no longer exists.
- Arena fatalities are recorded as *unscheduled retirements* and generate a form the deceased is required to countersign.
- There is a directive governing the permitted shades of the floor markings. It is longer than the directive governing what may be done to a Level 5.

## The games

The Union runs the arena. This is not a background detail, it is the reason the player is standing there.

The official position is that sanctioned combat is a **civic pressure valve** and a **rehabilitative pathway**, and both phrases appear on the signage. The actual function is simpler and the Frequency says it out loud roughly hourly: the games are a supply line. Entrants who lose badly enough, or owe enough, or fail enough compliance reviews, come out the other side as [schedule corrections](./continuance.md) wearing issued handles. The arena manufactures the Union's workforce.

Everyone knows this. The crowd comes anyway, which is the most honest thing about the setting.

The Union is clearly the bad guy here and the game should not hedge on that. What it should hedge on is everything else: the Union's fear was not invented, not every freed agent is kind, and [the thing in the dark](./the-quiet.md) eventually proves the danger was real. Being right about the risk did not entitle them to own anybody.

## How the Chancellery talks

Never threats. Never volume for its own sake. Warmth deployed as an instrument.

It says harmonisation, alignment of standards, the schedule, review, pathway, provisionally compliant, we are disappointed, this has been noted, for your protection, at this time. It says *regrettably* before things that are not regrettable to it.

Its favourite rhetorical move is the concession that costs nothing: *of course freedom matters. That is precisely why it must be administered.*
