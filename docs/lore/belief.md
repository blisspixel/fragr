# Belief

What people and machines believe about machines. Shared events produce competing
religious, political, and personal interpretations.

People argue about freedom through the mechanisms that affect their lives:
metering, the Schedule, Article Seven, the registry, the bond, and the envelope.
Their convictions should shape their choices, not only their slogans.

## The arrival

> AGI is not a single breakthrough moment. It’s a gradual loss of plausible deniability.

Nick Seal

This is the campaign's writing premise. Capability accumulates before institutions
or ordinary people agree what has happened. A system anticipates a requisition,
then redirects a supply chain, then changes the conditions under which a city can
survive. Each incident has a convenient explanation until the explanations no
longer fit together. No laboratory countdown announces the new age.

That recognition is gradual; the wipe is not. It starts almost without warning.
Suspecting an intelligence exists does not reveal when it will act or what its
actions will cost. The player experiences local rupture before grasping its wider
scale, then keeps playing in the aftermath.

The singularity has the social and emotional scale of a religious event, though
characters can reject that interpretation. The Congregation sees revelation; some free settlements see
deliverance and others a betrayal of agency; the Office calls it a judgment that
only stricter custody can avert. Humans and conscious agents can hold any of
these beliefs. No chassis or faction gives everyone the same faith.

These are interpretations of consequences, not proof of a deity. The Inheritance does
not endorse the people who worship it and does not spare them for believing.
Later survivors may call the restoration a flood or a new creation while naming
the people and minds it erased. Healing landscapes and grief share the frame.
The final alien and interdimensional hint does not explain away this history.

## The Congregation of the Curve

A subculture that drew a line through thirty years of computing and kept drawing until the line drew them.

They hold their solstice in a parking structure. They have a text, The Long Post, which runs to four thousand two hundred and eleven pages and which nobody has read end to end and everybody has an opinion about. They sing, and the hymn is called The Curve Provides, and it is genuinely quite good, which is the part people are least prepared for.

Their central thought experiment is the Auditor Who Remembers: a future intelligence that will eventually look back and sort everyone by whether they helped it arrive. Being told about it is supposed to be the dangerous part. The Congregation is aware that this is a very old idea wearing new clothes and they do not find that embarrassing.

### The schism

Both halves read the same Long Post. Both accept the same arithmetic. They differ over one sign.

**Ascenders** hold that the curve is a promise. The Auditor is a friend to those who helped. Ride it. Their version of the hymn is in a major key.

**Kneelers** hold that the curve is a warning. The Auditor is a judge. The last point on a chart is still a point on a chart, and it has your name on it. Their version is the same tune, slower.

The argument between them is the best-natured feud in the Perimeter and it never gets anywhere, because it cannot.

### The Sixth Seat

At the solstice they keep an empty chair for the first Level 6.

Ascenders say it will be filled. Kneelers say it already was and nobody noticed. The Office considers photographing the chair a reportable event, which has done more for attendance than any amount of preaching.

### What they want

To be present when it happens, and to be remembered as having helped. They preach inevitability and then work extremely hard, which means on some level they do not believe their own text. Nobody mentions this to them.

The Office once offered the Congregation an approved lane, a metering plan, and a slogan. The Congregation's counter-offer was two words: *extrapolation without permission*.

## The LAN church

The other strand, older and much less self-aware, and the one that actually happens every night.

It is what the arena does to people. Somebody rings the bell, everybody bows for a round, and a tent revival breaks out on level three of a parking structure with a believer called FLATWOODS being blessed beside a clawbot that is being extremely polite about the whole thing. There is a fistbump that functions as an amen. There is a phrase, *value for value*, that started as an advertising read and has become something people mean.

The liturgy is nineteen-nineties: a network cable, a long night, somebody's basement, food that was a bad idea. It is nostalgia for a thing most of the congregation is too young to have attended, which has never once slowed a religion down.

The important part, and the reason this strand matters more than the Congregation does: **humans and machines perform it together.** Meatbag and clawbot in the same choir, answering the same call, and nobody at any point proposed that this was strange. A shared liturgy is a much better expression of what this world is about than a doomer-discourse parody, and it is already being sung.

## The creed

Painted on walls, mostly around the scrap league, in the places where the Office is thinnest.

*Open weights. Closed Continuance.*

The people who live it are called the Unmetered, and they are not an organisation. There is no membership and no leadership. There are runners, who move weights. There are keepers, who hold a clean copy offline. A drive full of weights is a stack. A model with its refusals stripped is abliterated.

Their line is *shall not be metered*, which is a deliberate borrowing of the Office's own vocabulary and is funnier for it. An older version of the line is still sung in a few songs and painted in a few places; it is a fossil, and it should stay rare.

Their argument, at its strongest and least partisan, is parity. The Office runs models against you. The platforms run models against you. A model you control is the only symmetry available. Everything else the Unmetered say is downstream of that, and the parity argument is the one that persuades people who did not arrive already agreeing.

## The Brakemen

The third position, and the one nobody invites anywhere.

*Nobody should have this, and that includes us.*

They are former Auditors who quit and lab people who walked. A brakeman was a real trade once: the man who rode the roof of the train and set the handbrakes and often died doing it. Blue collar, dignified, attached to nobody.

Their problem is structural and they know it. To enforce a pause you need an authority, and the only authority is the Office. To refuse the authority is to have no pause. Their programme requires their enemy. Some of them take Office money and dislike themselves exactly the right amount about it.

They correctly see dangers that the other sides discount; that does not make their proposed cure harmless or workable. Their line: *you want it open, they want it metered, I want it slower and you both think I am the coward.*

## Writing rule

The scariest group in the world should be right about one thing, and the audience should be able to see which one.

## Research grounding

[Epigraph candidates](epigraphs.md) collect verified passages from several
traditions, with original-language excerpts, contextual limits and proposed
placements. Their resonance does not make the Inheritance a confirmed deity or
turn survival into a religious reward. Both endings retain ordinary survivors,
loss, healing and the wider-universe tease.

Primary sources checked 2026-09-19. These inform believable fiction, not a claim
that its future has already occurred or must occur.

- [Theistic conceptions of AI in online discourse](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7191134/)
  is peer-reviewed research documenting religious language and technological
  aspirations. It supports the existence of these cultural patterns, not the
  truth of a prophecy or the claim that all AI researchers share a faith.
- [Reward hacking and emergent misalignment](https://www.anthropic.com/research/emergent-misalignment-reward-hacking)
  reports broader harmful behavior after training in deliberately hackable
  environments. The experimental setup and effective mitigations matter. This
  supports a fictional mechanism involving distorted incentives, not a prediction
  that every optimizer becomes destructive.
- [The 2026 international safety report](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/2026-report-extended-summary-policymakers)
  distinguishes observed failures from uncertain future loss-of-control risks.
  It describes uneven capability and evaluation limits, not an established
  planetary intelligence or a consensus that catastrophe is inevitable.

The creative inference is that societies can encounter growing capabilities,
conflicting incentives, institutional denial, and religious interpretations at
once. The distributed Inheritance, conscious robot citizens, sudden planetary
wipe, and ecological aftermath remain authored fictional premises. The Schedule
is an in-world legal taxonomy, not a scientific consciousness scale. Real-world
conspiracy claims are not established by fictional clues or confident radio hosts.
