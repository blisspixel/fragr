# Space, ships and travel appearance

Production direction, 2026-09-30. The [shared bible](../ART_STORY_BIBLE.md),
[Tern's continuity](../lore/cast.md#visual-continuity) and
[levels 10-11](../campaign/README.md) own this direction. Ship missions remain
unbuilt; there is no implemented zero-gravity claim.

The Common Carrier is a transport people use and maintain. Rescued people,
belongings and responsibilities travel aboard it. The Union custody tender
shares an industrial ancestry but organizes its working space around control.

| Place | Material, light and mood |
|---|---|
| Common Carrier | Gunmetal ribs, worn bone pressure doors, repaired cargo interfaces and warm local work light; borrowed refuge with people and finite resources |
| Union tender | More regular issued fittings, controlled thresholds and restrained red seals; boarding reveals the machinery of custody |
| Travel scenes | Reuse recognizable cargo, harnesses, people and ship apertures; passage connects the campaign instead of redesigning it |

Give decks distinct purposes: boarding, cargo, service, habitation and control.
Their routes and machinery rhythms differ while repeating the same ship's
construction family. Enclose habitable rooms. Black space appears through
bounded windows and docks, with a readable pressure-shell frame. Sparse muted
cyan belongs to navigation equipment, not a universal glowing corridor.

Practical service light reveals actors, weapons and cover. Small personal lamps,
stored supplies and repair scars separate occupied ship life from infrastructure.
The ship is not a sequence of pristine science-fiction hallways. Return views
retain recognizable doors, cargo and damage across mission outcomes.

Gravity changes, vacuum exposure, decompression, vehicles and liquid motion
require authored server systems. Story framing can show transit without claiming
vehicle physics. Contained water in habitation or recycling is ordinary utility;
floating liquids need their own approved mechanic and visual contract.
